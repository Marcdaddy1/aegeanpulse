You probably don’t need a chatbot

Hi,

We build chatbots. So this is an odd thing to say: most of the small businesses that ask us for one don’t need it — at least not first, and sometimes not at all.

Plus, at the bottom: the AI myth I’m retiring this issue.

The argument

“Get a chatbot” is usually a solution that arrived before anyone defined the problem. The honest test is simple: what work does it take off a human, and how much of that work is there?

An e-commerce shop fielding hundreds of “where’s my order” messages a week? Real job, pays for itself. A consultancy getting dozens of considered enquiries? You’ve just put a robot in front of your best sales channel.

The friction is nearly always somewhere else — in the back office, where nobody can see it. Chasing follow-ups. Copying between systems. Rebuilding the same report by hand. That work doesn’t photograph well, so it never gets automated.

Myth of the issue

“We’d need to train an AI on our own data.”

Almost never. Training — properly, fine-tuning a model on your material — is expensive, slow, and goes stale the day your prices change.

What nearly every small business actually wants is retrieval: the model reads your documents at the moment someone asks. Update the document, the answer updates. No retraining, and it works on the files you already have.
