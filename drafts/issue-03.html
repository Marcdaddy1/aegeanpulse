Why AI hasn’t made your build quote cheaper

Hi,

AI writes code now. So why hasn’t the price of building software collapsed? A research benchmark published last week gives the clearest answer I’ve seen.

Plus, at the bottom: the AI pricing myth HubSpot just disproved.

The argument

The test gave coding agents a working web app and asked them to rebuild parts of it — closer to a real brief than the usual “follow this spec”. Two of the leading agents completed 49.2% and 28.8% of full rebuilds.

The telling part is the drop-off. Restore a small piece of a working app and the agents succeeded 100% and 96% of the time. Restore a larger, more connected part and that fell to 64% and 32%.

That’s a real build in miniature. The booking form appears in twenty minutes. The double bookings, refunds, timezones and no-shows are the job — and they’re exactly where the agents fell down. The screen got cheap. What happens behind it didn’t.

Myth of the issue

“Cheaper AI means a smaller bill.”

HubSpot cut the price of its AI agents in April. By August, monthly credit consumption had more than doubled. Cheaper units didn’t shrink the bill — they removed the friction that was keeping usage down.

Your tools are heading the same way: HubSpot is moving to a mix of seats and credits, and it won’t be the last. The fix is dull and it works — set a monthly cap on every tool you pay for by usage, and look at the usage once a month rather than once a year.
